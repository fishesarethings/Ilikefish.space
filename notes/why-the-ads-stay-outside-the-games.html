Why the ads stay outside the games

Notes · 4 November 2026

The site is funded by advertising, but the games themselves are not an advertising surface. That is a deliberate rule, not an accident of layout.

The rule

No ad appears inside a game. The game runs in its own frame, and the advertising code refuses to run there. There are no interstitials between levels, no pop-ups, no anchor ads stuck to the bottom of the screen and no rewarded video in the middle of a run.

Outside the game, a page may carry one advertising unit. It is labelled Advertisement, it sits below the game and the written guide, and it collapses if no ad is returned. If the unit is empty, you see nothing rather than a blank box.

What that looks like on a page

A typical game page is: the game, a short guide, controls, tips, a few questions, links to other games, and then the advertising unit. The ad never pushes the game off the screen and never covers it. On a phone, the same order holds.

The home page and the games index have one unit each, placed between content sections. Policy pages such as the privacy policy, terms, contact page and the offline page carry no advertising at all.

Consent comes first

Advertising and analytics are off by default. Nothing non-essential is requested until you make a choice in the consent banner. Visitors in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland see Google's certified consent message, and the choice can be changed later from the cookie settings link in the footer.

The site does not infer consent from the presence of a script. It waits for an actual choice, and it can withdraw ad requests if consent is taken away.

What is not on the site

  • No auto-play video ads.
  • No pop-unders or new-window ads.
  • No ads disguised as buttons, menus or game controls.
  • No "download" or "install" prompts attached to advertising.
  • No ads on the pages that explain the rules.

Why it matters

Games are short. An interstitial in a two-minute game is not a pause; it is most of the session. Keeping advertising outside the frame is the difference between a site you can use and a site that uses you.

It also makes the site easier to review. A page has one job, the game has one job, and the advertising has one clearly marked place. The Privacy Policy describes what is collected and how to change your choice.
